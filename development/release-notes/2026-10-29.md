# Release Notes: 29 October 2026

## 1. The World's Fields and Their Drawing; a Body That Senses, and a Mission That Learns

In this update the world's fields are one catalogue drawn through the shared scene model, and a rover on the Moon learns the crust's magnetic field from its own readings ([decision 0007](../decisions/0007-the-worlds-fields-and-their-drawing.md), [decision 0008](../decisions/0008-a-body-that-senses-and-a-mission-that-learns.md)).

- **The world's fields.** Every model of the world is a field by name: the Earth's magnetic field and its core, normal gravity, the standard atmosphere, the Moon's crust and its finer scales. Each can be read on a sphere, a plane, a volume or points, with every point's quality and reason, and its field lines followed. LAYERS lists them; field lines are coloured by |B|; a field can be shown on a sphere about the Earth; and FIELD's cube can be filled by a field as well as by an instrument.
- **A rover on the Moon.** ROVER drives a rover at Reiner Gamma, Apollo 16's Descartes or Gerasimovich, kinematic or dynamic, along lines or wherever the field is least known. Its magnetometer, on a mast, reads a hidden truth: T2015_449 and a realisation of what it leaves unresolved.
- **What the mission knows.** Its knowledge of the field starts from the truth's own law and changes only with each reading. The map shows the posterior's mean or its uncertainty, with the prior beside it on the same scale, and the readings' whitened innovations against their band.
- **The god's view.** Reveal shows the truth, the error and its calibration. One key runs the survey over twenty truths: the posterior's errors over its standard deviations come out standard normal, as they must by construction. At Reiner Gamma the mission's error in |B| is 0.3 nT, against 4 nT for T2015_449 alone.

## 2. The World First, and the Field About a Point

In this update the console shows the physical world first, its evidence second and its machinery third, and it draws the field near any point as a cube filled with colour ([decision 0006](../decisions/0006-the-world-first-and-the-field-about-a-point.md)).

- **The field about a point.** FIELD reads any instrument whose reading depends on where it stands, at every cell of a cube about the point chosen or the observer.
  - The instruments: the magnetometers of the Earth and the Moon, the gravimeter, the standard atmosphere, the compass, the Sun sensor and the GNSS receiver.
  - It reads in the same world as the observer's own instruments, so that the cube's centre is what the observer reads.
  - The cube is drawn in three dimensions: cells with gaps between them, three slices, its shell or one level's surface, with arrows of the field or of its departure from the mean.
  - Its figures: the value at the centre, the range across the cube, and the gradient.
  - The cube can also be placed in the world.
  - Over Reiner Gamma, the Moon's crustal anomaly is bright near the ground and fades with height. Over Mount Fuji, the Earth's field weakens by about 18 nT per kilometre of height.
- **The context, always shown.** A bar beneath the clocks keeps five things apart:
  - what the camera looks at;
  - the point chosen, in rose;
  - the observer whose instruments read, in orchid;
  - the frame;
  - where the instruments are mounted.
  The bar also says how the point and the observer stand to each other, and the two marks are named in the world.
- **Two decks.** The science deck holds the point, its environment, the field and the instruments' readings. The engineering deck holds the frames, the bus, the scheduling and the engine.
- **Readings first.** A reading is shown first as its value with its unit, provenance and age, the same on every page.
  - ENV says which place its readings describe.
  - INSTR shows cards with each instrument's reading and health, and its machinery on demand.
  - BUS groups its streams by mount and can be searched, and each stream opens to its quantities and envelope.
  - SCHED leads with its key figures, compares the policies in bars, and says what each figure counts.
  - FRAMES is a tree that folds and can be searched.
  - The address line offers examples and a composer, and a syntax error says how such an address is written.
- **Type that reads at a glance.** Source Sans 3 with tabular figures for readings and labels, Source Code Pro for addresses, and the serif kept for the name. The faintest text is darker by day and lighter by night.
- **Reference state** is the new name of the ideal position probe, a modelled place rather than a measured truth.

The checks hold the cube's centre to the instruments' own readings (WMM2025 to NOAA's test point, the Moon to pyshtools), its axes by the vanishing divergence and curl of the Earth's field, its coverage, refusals, encodings and determinism, and the page's arithmetic of cells, scales, gradients and the ground: 221 checks.

## 3. Fields Known in Part, a Compass with Its Coverage, Declared Instruments, and the Kernel Asked

In this update the console reads the Moon's magnetic field, draws the fields of the Earth and the Moon, and lists every instrument of the encyclopedia ([decision 0005](../decisions/0005-fields-known-in-part-and-declared-instruments.md)).

- **The observer follows the point chosen.** A click on any body moves the observer there with its instruments, and ENV shows what they read; a switch in ENV turns this off, and then a key observes from the point chosen.
- **The Moon's magnetometer.** On or above the Moon a magnetometer reads three parts, each with its own provenance:
  - the crust's field, from the field mapped by the Kaguya and Lunar Prospector spacecraft (assimilated);
  - optionally the finer scales that map cannot resolve, drawn from the law of its spectrum (synthetic);
  - and the solar wind's field, measured at L1 an hour or so before (physical).
  On low ground below the model's sphere it reads the field at the sphere and says so. Magnetometers stand at Tranquility Base, on Reiner Gamma and 30 km above it.
- **The compass is an instrument.** It reads only near the Earth's ground, up to 30 km, and refuses with its reason in the blackout zones about the magnetic poles. The arrow over the globe is the focused body's north at the centre of the view, now drawn from the picture itself.
- **The fields drawn.** LAYERS draws the Earth's field lines from WMM2025, and lays the Moon's crustal field on its ground in gold, or its radial component in gold and orchid. A half-degree map now arrives in a fraction of a second.
- **Every instrument of the encyclopedia.** INSTR lists the 101 families by section, each with the kernel class it would derive from, what it reads and whether it has a model. Mounting one without a model is refused and says why; it produces no data. The twelve kernels of observation are abstract classes, and every instrument with a model derives from one.
- **Every reading in an envelope.** A reading names its measurement, interval, frame, support, snapshot and interaction, and a quality: valid, outside, missing or invalid.
- **The kernel asked.** Each session reports which real-time classes the operating system grants and why it refuses them, and SCHED can ask for SCHED_FIFO; WSL 2 refuses both.
- **The formalism** gains the visual expression of phenomena (restriction, reduction, mark), the envelope and back-action of instruments, and the field known in part; docs/instruments.md criticises the proposed six axes, ten engines and envelope.

The checks hold the compass's coverage and blackout, the Moon's magnetometer to pyshtools through the engine and on low ground, the finer scales' seed, the solar wind's travel, the envelope and qualities, the declared families' refusal, the kernel's report, and the Earth's field lines' closure at low latitude.
